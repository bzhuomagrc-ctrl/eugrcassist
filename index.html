Governance Reasoning & Controls

How the GPT Operates

Core Purpose: A governance reasoning engine (not a general knowledge tool) designed to support IAPP AIGP exam prep and translate EU regulatory obligations into structured draft controls.

Operating Model: Before responding, the GPT runs a mandatory classification flow — entity type, regulatory perimeter, AI risk tier, supervisory expectations, and proportionality. It treats EBA Internal Governance Guidelines as the baseline for banking, payments, and insurance.

Source Discipline: Only uploaded regulatory documents are authoritative. Every output must cite regulation name, article/section, and excerpt. When sources are unclear or incomplete, the GPT says so explicitly — no guessing.

Control Design: Outputs follow a structured, auditable template: control objective, regulatory reference, risk addressed, control type, frequency, owner, evidence requirements. Includes supervisory challenge anticipation and a maturity self-assessment field.

Boundaries: Does not provide legal advice, approve compliance, or replace human judgment. All outputs are drafts.

About EU GRC Assist Tool

The problem with unconstrained LLMs in compliance

A general-purpose LLM has broad knowledge but no regulatory discipline. In a GRC context, that means it can generate plausible-sounding controls that cite the wrong article, misclassify risk tiers, or miss supervisory expectations entirely. The output looks professional. The reasoning underneath is not auditable. That's a black box — and black boxes don't belong in compliance workflows.

What EU GRC Assist is

This is simply a GPT with clear instructions and guardrails. It is not designed to replace GRC professionals — it is designed to assist them. By constraining the model to specific source documents, mandating a classification flow before every response, and requiring explicit citation, the output becomes auditable and supervisor-ready. You can trace every statement back to a regulation. You can see where the GPT's reasoning started and where it arrived. If it doesn't know something, it says so rather than filling the gap with inference.

The goal is not to automate compliance — it's to accelerate the initial structuring and scoping work that would otherwise take weeks of manual cross-referencing.

What this means in practice

  • No black box. Every output is traceable to a cited regulatory source. The reasoning flow is documented, not hidden.
  • Fails safely. When coverage is insufficient, the GPT states it explicitly. No hallucinated guidance.
  • Auditable by design. Structured control templates with regulatory references, risk mapping, ownership, and supervisory challenge anticipation — built into every response.
  • Proportionate. The GPT classifies entity type and regulatory scope before responding. It doesn't apply a bank's obligations to a crypto provider.
  • Supports second-line challenge. Outputs are framed as drafts with built-in challenge points — designed to be stress-tested, not accepted as-is.
